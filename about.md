## Who we are

Drive-in Lab is a research group at the **{{ site.organisation }}**, based in the **{{ site.faculty }}**. We study human inattention in traffic — why drivers look away from the road, how modern in-car information and entertainment systems compete for attention, and how this attention allocation relates to crash risk.

The lab is run by Professor [Tuomo Kujala]({{ '/people/tuomo-kujala/' | relative_url }}). He is a Full professor in Cognitive Science at the University of Jyväskylä, and leads the group's work with the Drive-in Laboratory.

## The Drive-in Laboratory

The lab's defining piece of infrastructure is the **Drive-in Laboratory**,

built in early 2024: a 6 m × 7.2 m container housing a full-scale driving simulation. Any car can be driven directly into the facility and connected to the simulation, which projects a 180° traffic scene in front of the vehicle (three projectors, 5760×1200) and a rear scene visible through the mirrors (1920×1080). This lets researchers run controlled, repeatable traffic scenarios around a real production vehicle and measure how its infotainment and driver-assistance systems affect driver attention.

## Research themes

- **Driver inattention** — modeling when and why attention lapses occur, and what makes certain moments more dangerous than others.
- **In-car infotainment distraction** — measuring and rating how OEM touchscreen and entertainment systems draw attention from driving.
- **Human-machine interaction in vehicles** — designing and evaluating interfaces that support, rather than compete with, safe driving.
- **Context-sensitive risk prediction** — building models that anticipate when inattention is likely to lead to a safety-critical event.
